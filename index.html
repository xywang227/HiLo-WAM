Two temporal scales, one coordinated policy

HiLo-WAM decouples long-horizon task evolution and local visual dynamics within a shared Video DiT, coupled to an Action DiT. Multi-scale temporal modeling and structured cross-stream interaction coordinate both streams, while progressive inference reduces unnecessary computation.

Complementary temporal modeling

Sparse history and future evolution capture long-horizon task progression; the dense local stream models fine-grained robot–object interactions for action generation.

02

Coordinated, efficient inference

Structured cross-stream attention coordinates temporal scales. Partial denoising, joint denoising, and dynamic skipping reduce redundant computation.

Strong manipulation performance

Simulation and real-robot evaluations validate complementary temporal information, the coverage–resolution trade-off, and 2.93× faster inference.

Attention mask, notation, and training details

Structured cross-stream attention

The long-horizon stream attends bidirectionally within itself. Local and action tokens access historical context and the nearest coarse future latent; distant future latents provide indirect guidance only. Actions also attend to local dynamics and robot state. Visual tokens do not attend to actions.

Z denotes latent tokens; H / L / A identify the long-horizon / local / action streams.

ZH,ctxt
Long-horizon historical context
ZH,neart
Nearest coarse future latent frame
ZH,fart
Distant coarse future latent frames
ZLt
Local visual dynamics tokens
qt / ZAt
Robot state / action tokens at time t

Joint flow matching uses independently sampled flow times for the long-horizon, local, and action streams. Historical observations, the current observation, and robot state remain clean conditions.

Reported configuration: Wan2.2-5B video backbone; stride k = 4; 8 historical and 8 future keyframes; video horizon T = 32; action horizon H = 32. Progressive inference uses 5 / 5 / 10 denoising budgets for long-horizon / local / action streams, starts the streams jointly, and skips redundant DiT evaluations when consecutive velocity predictions are sufficiently similar. The action stream retains its full 10-step schedule.

2.93× faster inference, with improved success

Three complementary strategies reduce action-chunk latency from 1114.46 to 380.02 ms, while RoboTwin 2.0 success improves from 93.54% to 93.70%.

  1. Partial denoising

    Stop each visual stream at its budget and cache its features; keep the full action denoising schedule.

  2. Joint denoising

    Start all streams together so visual and action representations interact at aligned denoising stages.

  3. Dynamic skipping

    Reuse sufficiently similar consecutive velocity predictions to skip redundant DiT evaluations.

Real-world manipulation

We evaluate three bimanual tasks on Fourier GR-3: Build House, Disassemble House, and Disassemble Heart.

OOD evaluation changes lighting, tabletop background, and clutter. Each task uses 10 trials per setting, with a 0–10 progress score and separate full-task success rate. HiLo-WAM achieves the highest average progress and success among the three compared models: 6.9 / 33% in-domain and 6.0 / 30% OOD.

Limitations

Performance remains bounded by the video backbone’s semantic understanding and predictive capability. Explicit subtask decomposition, progress estimation, and higher-level reasoning remain challenging.
